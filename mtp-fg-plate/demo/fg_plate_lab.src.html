How the work was checked

  • FSDT and TSDT solvers match an independent symbolic derivation to about 10⁻¹⁴, and published TSDT frequencies to all 4 printed digits.
  • 27 mechanics checks and 35 unit tests pass; the 200-design dataset is frozen and checksummed.
  • Confirmations E4, E5, H2 and H3 were each pre-registered and evaluated once on designs generated after the plan was locked.
  • Every experiment was rerun and reproduced bit for bit.
Scope. Simply supported rectangular plates, linear elasticity, smooth loads. The accurate reference is TSDT (a plate theory), standing in for FEM or 3D analysis; costs are counted in accurate analyses, not seconds.

How it works

The idea in four steps: learn the cheap physics without data, spend the few expensive solutions only on what the physics gets wrong, and always check the final design.

STEP 1 · NO DATA

Learn the cheap physics

A polynomial model of each plate mode's response is fitted to the FSDT energy equations at 1,000+ unlabelled designs. No solved examples are needed.

STEP 2 · 10 SOLUTIONS

Learn only the correction

FSDT misses higher-order shear effects in thick plates. That error grows like (k·h)², so the correction is learned in that form from 10 TSDT solutions.

STEP 3 · INSTANT

Predict any design and load

Bending fields and the first frequency for any g, a/h, b/a, and any smooth load, in microseconds.

STEP 4 · ONE CHECK

Design, then verify

Optimize the plate with the ML model, then check the chosen design once with the accurate theory. Corrections, when needed, take one more check.

The model

HML(x) = Hphysics(x) · [1 + (k h)² · p(x)]
  • x = (g, a/h, b/a), the design; H = the plate's response to a unit load in one sine mode.
  • Hphysics is trained on FSDT energy only; it reproduces FSDT to 0.03%.
  • (k h)² is zero for thin plates, so the correction cannot spoil them; p(x) is a small polynomial fitted to 10 TSDT solutions chosen where shear matters most.
  • Any load is a sum of sine modes, so one model handles every load shape.

The two plate theories

  • FSDT (first-order shear deformation): fast, uses a shear correction factor 5/6; a few percent off for thick graded plates.
  • TSDT (Reddy third-order): cubic displacement through the thickness, no correction factor; our accurate reference, standing in for FEM or 3D elasticity.
  • Material: Al₂O₃ (E = 380 GPa, ρ = 3800 kg/m³) to Al (70 GPa, 2702 kg/m³), power-law grading Vc = (1/2 + z/h)g, ν = 0.3.
  • Supports: simply supported on all edges (Navier double-sine solution).

What is not covered yet

  • Other boundary conditions (clamped edges) and irregular shapes, which need FEM.
  • Validation against FEM, 3D elasticity or experiments; TSDT itself is a plate theory.
  • Point loads, thermal loads, buckling and nonlinear deformation.
  • A neural-network version of the same physics loss (the polynomial form was chosen for a clean comparison).
